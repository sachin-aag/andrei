5.0 DEFINITIONS:
5.1 Quality Risk Management:
A systematic process for the assessment, control, communication, implementation and review of
risks to the quality of the drug product across the product lifecycle.
5.2 Risk:
The combination of the probability of occurrence of harm and the severity of that harm.

A systematic process of organizing information to support a risk decision. It consists of the
identification of hazards and the analysis and evaluation of risks associated with exposure to those
hazards.

Review or monitoring of output/results of the risk management process considering (if appropriate)

Risk Priority Number (RPN):
A quantitative method for determining the level of risk by multiplying the severity, probability of
occurrence and detectability rankings of the failure or event.

5.18 Risk Priority Ranking (RPR):
A qualitative method for determining the level of risk by combining severity, occurrence and
detectability rankings of the failure or event.

5.19 Residual Risk:
Risk remaining after risk control measures has been taken.

5.20 Validation:
Establishing documented evidence that provides a high degree of assurance that a specific process
will consistently produce a product meeting its predetermined specifications and quality attributes.

5.21 Direct Impact System:
Those Building /Facility / Utility / Equipment / Instrument / Process which controls and or sustain
a cGMP operation directly thereby impacting safety, efficacy and or purity of a product. (example :
Manufacturing block, Clean room, Facility to ensure flow of material and personnel to minimize
contamination, dedicated facilities / equipment, Purified water system, Filling machine, AHU
system for areas where product / materials are exposed during operation.)

5.22 Indirect Impact Systems:
Those Building / Facility / Utility / Equipment / Instrument / Process which do not control and or
sustain a cGMP operation directly, but still perceived to have an impact on safety, efficacy and or
purity of a product (e.g. : Boiler steam, Raw water system, Building Management System, access
control etc.).

5.23 Qualitative Risk Assessment (Informal):
Qualitative risk assessment shall be performed based on the level of risk ranking, as high, medium
and low. Risk priority ranking shall be evaluated in qualitative risk assessment.

5.24 Quantitative Risk Assessment (Formal):
Quantitative risk assessment shall be performed based on the level of risk rating. Risk priority
number shall be calculated in quantitative risk assessment.

Primary principles of quality risk management are:

The evaluation of the risk to quality should be based on scientific knowledge and ultimately link to
the patient safety.

The level of effort, formality and documentation of the quality risk management process should be
commensurate with the level of risk.

Risk shall be identified by assessing the following, but not limited to:

Equipment:
Type, design, capacity, location, installation, condition, lubricants used, equipment history,
operation, maintenance, calibration and breakdown etc.

Facilities:

Design, Site layout, material movement, personnel movement, waste movement, pest control,
utilities, building management system maintenance, building security measures, safety measures
and facility monitoring system etc.

Process:

Development, Introduction of new product, Process change and rework / reprocess. Any major
change or modification to the qualified facility, (Any Change) equipment, instrument, utility,
process, product, system etc.

Document system:

Master documents, standard operating procedure, validation reports, self-inspection reports, change
control system, deviation, CAPA, APQR, market complaint reports, batch manufacturing records,
batch packing records, equipment usage logs, analytical records and reports of raw materials,
packing materials, stability data, process/cleaning validation reports, environmental monitoring
records, rework or reprocess and batch manufacturing records etc.

Materials:

Identity, status control, quality, quantity, handling, specifications, security arrangements,
counterfeiting control, sampling, reference / working standards, out of specification, retention
samples, destruction of samples, analysis report, laboratory safety, retesting, storage, segregation

74 Risk Control — Mitigation plan, Risk reduction or Acceptance

TAA] Risk control includes decision making to reduce and/or accept risks. The purpose of risk control is
to reduce the risk to an acceptable level.

7.4.2 Risk control might focus on the following questions:

7.4.2.1 Is the risk above an acceptable level?

7.4.2.2 | What can be done to reduce or eliminate risks?

7.4.2.3 What is the appropriate balance among benefits, risks and resources?

7.4.2.4 Are new risks introduced as a result of the identified risks being controlled?

74.3 The Mitigation plan elaborates the current control and the actions to reduce the overall risk to an
acceptable levels or elimination.

TAA For cases where overall risk is “Low”, mitigation plan is not required and the risk assessment shall

be closed.

7.4.5 The mitigation plan is mandatory where overall risk is “Medium” and "High", controls relating to
the risk should be implemented as soon as practically possible.

7.4.6 — Mitigation plan for medium category risk shall be done to reduce the risk to an acceptable level.

7.4.7 Mitigation plan for high category risk shall be Identify and implement first to reduce the risk to an
acceptable level, before proceeding to the activity.

74.8 The mitigation plan shall briefly explain the recommended action to be taken; responsible person
shall be implementing the controls mentioned in the risk mitigation plan and the expected target
date for completion.
