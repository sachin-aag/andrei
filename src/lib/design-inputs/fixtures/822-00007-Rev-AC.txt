       1.1 822-00007 Solea Model 3 Software Requirements
       Introduction

       This requirements document is intended to provide a comprehensive description of the
       associated design input requirements to be addressed in the design and development process.

                  1. Purpose
             This document defines the requirements that will guide the design and development of the
             Solea system software.

             The primary purpose of the Solea software is to manage the overall operation of a cart
             based dental laser system called the Solea system. The Solea system is intended to be
             used for:

                        •   Ablation of hard tissue for caries removal and cavity preparation.
                        •   Incision, excision, vaporization, coagulation, and hemostasis of soft tissue
                            in the oral cavity.
                        • Cutting, shaving, contouring, and resection of oral osseous tissue (bone).
                        • Aid in the reduction of mineral loss in dental enamel.
             The level of concern for the Solea software is determined to be Moderate.

             The Solea system is depicted in Figure 1 below.

                 4. Terms and Definitions
             This section describes the terms and acronyms used in the software and documentation.



          Term                     Definition

          Laser Frequency          The frequency of the laser, which is directly related to the light
          (Wavelength)             wavelength. There is a specific laser frequency that has been
                                   proven to be effective at causing oscillation and disintegration of
                                   dental hard and soft tissue.

          Laser Pulse Duration     This is a measure of the “on time” at which the laser is lasing for
                                   a given pulse period. The duration of laser emission is short
                                   (microseconds) to control cutting and the potential of overheating
                                   and causing unintended damage to the organic material being
                                   “ablated” or causing damage to the material surrounding the
                                   target area.

          Jump Delay               Time to wait for the laser emission to decay before moving the
                                   aiming mirrors.

          Laser Energy             The laser pulse length and pulse rate are both varied in order to
                                   customize the energy delivered to the tissue being removed.

          Laser Cutting Pattern    The laser cuts (ablates) material by pulsing in a series of dots or
                                   points within a pattern. The dots/points are arranged so the laser
                                   fires and then moves away within the pattern for optimal cutting.

          Air/Water Mist           During laser cutting, a mist of air and water can be sprayed on
                                   the cutting area to provide cooling and remove debris.

          Spot Size                A number that specifies the diameter of the laser cutting pattern
                                   used to ablate dental material.

          Term                    Definition

          Single Pulse Spot       A type of pattern consisting of a single pulse per firing point.
          Size

          Laser Cutting Speed     The laser pulses for a given pattern at a duty-cycle depending on
                                  foot pedal position. As the foot pedal is further depressed, the
                                  laser pulse duration (pulse width) is increased for the given
                                  pattern. Longer pulse width results in an increased rate of cutting
                                  for a given spot size.

          Handpiece               The instrument the dentist holds to perform laser ablation. The
                                  handpiece can be used for cutting hard tissue or soft tissue. The
                                  system supports four types of handpieces; Contra Angle,
                                  Straight, TD, and Solea Protect handpiece.

          Aiming Beam             The Solea hardware provides an aiming mechanism that
                                  illuminates the position of where the CO2 laser will ablate
                                  (cut). Users can “point” the aiming beam to the desired area for
                                  cutting and then activate the CO2 laser to cut hard and soft
                                  tissue.

          Alignment               A periodic maintenance procedure in which the user adjusts the
                                  laser galvanometers “home” or center position so that the laser
                                  beam is centered in the laser aperture.

          Galvanometer            A device that has a mirror connected at its end that is used to
                                  deflect laser light. The galvanometer has an electrical control
          (or “galvo”)            interface that allows for precise movements of the attached
                                  mirror to allow positioning of the laser in either the x or y
                                  direction. Solea has two galvanometers for x and y direction
                                  control.

          Aperture                The opening at the end of the handpiece through which the laser
                                  light passes.

          Laser Controller        The laser controller controls the laser and the galvos to achieve
                                  ablation according to selected laser cutting pattern and foot pedal
                                  position.

                                  The software supports two types of laser controllers; the Lanmark
                                  Controller (LCD) and the Pattern Controller (PCON).

                                  PCON differs from the LCD in a few areas that affect the
                                  software:

                                       1. LCD hosts the patterns in persistent storage on the LCD,
                                          so a pattern file must be uploaded once and after that
                                          the host software will invoke the pattern through a
                                          specified command.
                                          PCON does not store the pattern information in
                                          persistent memory, therefore the host software is

       1.7.10 Alignment State Description (Perioguide Only)
       The alignment state consists of:
            • The setup phase, where user setups the Perioguide accessories (Perioguide
               Acquisition Module and Perioguide Sensor Module) and connects it to Solea.
               During this phase the laser is not being fired.
            • Alignment phase, the laser is being fired for a short period of time into the
               Perioguide Sensor Module. The software will determine the optimal alignment
               based on the sensor readings received from the Perioguide Sensor Module.
                   o The alignment phase consists of home position power check and
                       scanning phase.
                            ▪ Home position check - The system shall check the home galvo
                               position for sufficient power. If sufficient power is found at home
                               position, scanning phase will be skipped.

                             ▪ Scanning Phase - The system adjusts galvo positions,
                               attempting to determine peak power at a given galvo address.
            •   Result phase, where the user is informed of the alignment result.
